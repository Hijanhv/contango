The instrument

A margined forward book,
and a desk that owns the other side.

  • 01Forwards. Cash-settled USDC/EURC at listed weekly value dates. Post 8% margin, lock a rate, settle at expiry. Minimum ticket: $1.
  • 02The desk. The protocol is counterparty to every ticket, quoting around covered-interest-parity fair value with inventory-aware skew.
  • 03The vault. An ERC-4626 balance sheet that delta-hedges every forward in spot, so its P&L is spread + carry — never a directional FX bet.

04
The quant core — pricing

A forward isn't a forecast.
It's arithmetic.

No-arbitrage pins the forward rate exactly: spot, plus the cost of carrying two currencies to the value date.

F  =  S · (1 + rUSD · τ) / (1 + rEUR · τ)
S

USDC/EURC spot mid, medianised across two independent sources.

r_USD / r_EUR

Live SOFR and €STR fixings, posted on-chain by a rates keeper.

τ

ACT/360 year fraction — the money-market convention both rates quote in.

When r_USD > r_EUR the curve slopes upward — contango — and F − S is the forward points the desk earns for carrying the position. We named the protocol after the structure it builds.

05
The quant core — market making

The desk's own axe
moves the price.

Inventory-skewed quoting, Avellaneda–Stoikov-lite. The mid is pushed away from fair value in proportion to how one-sided the book already is:

mid = F · (1 + skew) ,   skew = −k · (inventory / max)

A crowded book makes the next ticket on that side pay up, and pays the other side to come flatten it. The off-chain quant engine layers its own alpha on top — but bounded, so it can never escape the on-chain risk rails.

On-chain

Deterministic inventory skew + half-spread. Always live, always sane, no operator required.

Off-chain

The quant engine's overlay, capped at ±20bps by the contract itself.

06
The quant core — the vault

USD yield from FX flow.
Not emissions.

Every forward written is immediately delta-hedged in spot through App Kit Swap. The directional FX cancels. What survives is a real FX swap desk's revenue:

Spread

Bid/ask captured around CIP fair value, booked on day one.

Carry

As τ → 0, forward points roll down to zero. Marking the book to the CIP curve makes this fall out of the accounting for free — it is the carry.

Margin fees

Charged on notional at open, accruing to the desk.

The vault holds three things and marks all of them continuously: idle USDC, the EURC hedge inventory, and its claim on the desk. Only their sum is the depositor's money — and that sum has no FX beta.

07
Why Arc, and only Arc

A forward is a promise that has to survive until expiry.

That is exactly why TradFi wraps it in credit. On Arc, three properties replace the credit line.

01 — Sub-second finality

Deterministic, non-reorgable. Real-time variation margin and liquidation that is final in the block it lands in. You can run a clearing house's risk engine every block.

02 — USDC-native gas

A margin top-up costs a known fraction of a cent. This is what collapses the minimum ticket from $1,000,000 to $1.

03 — Native USDC + EURC

Both legs settle atomically, 24/7 — including Saturday, when every TradFi desk on earth is closed.

The forward curve is the implied USD–EUR rate differential. Arc doesn't have one. We built it.

09
Risk machinery

The margin engine is
the whole product.

  • IM8% initial / 4% maintenance, sized off the 99th-percentile weekly EURUSD move — because the desk cannot make a margin call by phone at 3am on a Sunday.
  • ORAMedianised dual-source oracle with a disagreement guard. If the feeds diverge, the engine stops opening and stops liquidating, rather than trusting either print.
  • CBCircuit breaker on any single-update move beyond 5%. Real gaps get walked in; garbage prints get rejected and escalated.
  • LIQPermissionless keeper liquidations, final in one block. The keeper race is a race to a settled outcome, not to a reorg.
  • INVCash accounting identity, asserted by the invariant suite: every USDC in the engine is either trader margin or desk cash. Bad debt is surfaced, never hidden.
